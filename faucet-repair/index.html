What a failing faucet looks like at home

Most faucets give warning signs for weeks before they fail. The sign you notice usually points to one specific part, which is why describing it well saves time on the call.

Drip from the spout

A steady drip after the handle is off usually means a worn washer, seat or cartridge seal inside the body.

Water at the base

Moisture pooling around the base of the faucet points to a cracked O-ring or a loose packing nut.

Handle feels loose or gritty

Mineral scale or a stripped set screw makes the handle wobble, grind or turn too far.

Weak or uneven flow

A clogged aerator or a half-closed supply valve often causes low pressure at only one fixture.

Hot and cold mix wrongly

A worn mixing cartridge lets hot and cold bleed together, so the temperature drifts while you wash.

Knob spins without turning on

A stripped stem or broken handle insert on a tub or shower knob leaves you unable to open or close the water.

Which part is likely failing

Use this as a rough map. A technician confirms the cause with the water off, because two parts can fail together.

Water will not stop or is spraying

A faucet or valve that cannot be shut off can put gallons per minute onto a floor and into the ceiling below. Call our 24/7 line right away. Same-day and after-hours visits are available for active leaks, and a small bathroom or kitchen can take on real damage within a few hours.

  1. Turn the angle stop under the fixture clockwise until it stops.
  2. If there is no stop or it will not turn, close the main shutoff at the meter or where the line enters the house.
  3. Open one low faucet to drain the pressure from the pipes.
  4. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet area.
  5. Keep people away from outlets or appliances sitting in standing water.

Supply valves, stops and the shutoffs behind a fixture

The small valve under a sink is called an angle stop or supply valve. It lets you cut water to one fixture without losing the whole house. Older stops often seize from mineral buildup and weep around the stem, or snap when forced. We replace them with quarter-turn stops that open and close with a short twist.

A broken stop is also the reason many simple repairs turn into bigger jobs. If you cannot isolate the fixture, we shut off at the main, cut out the old valve and sweat or compress-fit a new one. Valves behind washing machines and fridge lines get the same treatment.

How the repair visit runs

  1. Describe the leak

    Tell dispatch which fixture, where the water shows up and whether the handle still moves.

  2. Isolate the fixture

    The technician closes the stop or main so the work happens on a dry, unpressurized line.

  3. Strip and inspect

    Handle, cap and cartridge come out. We look for scale, nicks on the seat and a cracked body.

  4. Match the parts

    Cartridges and seats are not universal, so we match the maker and model before swapping anything.

  5. Test under pressure

    We reopen the water, run hot and cold, and check every joint dry with a paper towel.

Shower valves, tub spouts and outdoor spigots

Shower and tub valves hide behind the wall, so the visible handle is only the control. When a shower drips from the head even though it is off, the cartridge or the diverter inside the valve is usually worn. If the knob spins freely, the stem or handle insert is stripped. We can often replace the trim parts without opening tile.

Outdoor spigots fail for different reasons. A hose left on in winter can freeze and split the pipe inside the wall, and the leak only shows when the spigot is opened in spring. If water appears inside when you run the hose, shut off the spigot's interior valve and call us. Where a leak could be inside a wall, our leak detection service can trace it.

What sets the cost of a faucet repair

A drip fixed with one cartridge is a very different job from a corroded valve behind a finished wall. You get the price in plain terms before the first tool comes out.

Repair or replace

Swapping a cartridge costs less than a new faucet, but a worn body can make the repair a poor use of money.

Part availability

Common cartridges are quick to source. Discontinued or imported fixtures can take extra time to match.

Valve access

A stop under an open cabinet is simple. A shower valve behind tile or drywall needs an access plan.

Condition of nearby pipe

Corroded or brittle supply lines may need replacing so the new part does not fail beside old pipe.

After-hours timing

Night and weekend calls are priced for the visit, and we say so on the phone first.

Home insurance often covers sudden water damage from a burst supply line, but it rarely pays to fix a worn cartridge. Read your own policy and ask your agent before you file.

What the technician looks at on arrival

Before we replace anything, we confirm the cause. These checks tell us whether a part swap will hold.

  • ✓Water pressure at the fixture, to see whether high pressure is wearing out seals.
  • ✓The angle stop and supply line for seepage, mineral crust or green corrosion.
  • ✓The cartridge or washer face for scoring and flat spots.
  • ✓The faucet body and deck for hairline cracks.
  • ✓Drain and overflow connections, since a damp cabinet is not always the faucet.
  • ✓Nearby fixtures, such as a garbage disposal or dishwasher line, that may share the leak.

Why hard water ruins faucets slowly

The Northern Kentucky Water District's 2023 report shows about 7 grains per gallon across its systems, which is moderately hard to hard. Over time that scale builds inside cartridges and aerators, so handles stiffen and flow drops. If your faucet feels gritty, a repair plus a vinegar soak on the aerator can restore it, and a worn cartridge is cheaper to replace than the whole fixture.
