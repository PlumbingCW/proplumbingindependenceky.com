Slow, gurgling, or standing water: what each sign means

Drains rarely fail without warning. They give hints for weeks, and the hint depends on where the blockage sits in the pipe.

One sink drains slowly

Hair, soap film, or grease is narrowing the trap or the short branch behind it. A hand cable usually clears this in one visit.

Gurgling after a flush

Air is being pulled past a partial clog, or the vent stack is restricted. The sound is a warning that water is struggling to leave.

Tub rises when the washer runs

The washer pumps out a lot of water quickly. If a lower shared line is narrowed, the lowest drain in the house shows it first.

Toilet and shower both slow

Two fixtures on different branches sharing a symptom means the blockage is probably past where the branches meet, in the main drain.

Kitchen sink backs into the laundry sink

Both fixtures feed the same line. The clog sits downstream of where they join, often a grease buildup in a 2-inch pipe.

Water drains, then comes back

A partial clog lets water slip by at first. As the pipe fills, pressure pushes some of it back up the way it came.

Water is coming up out of a drain right now

Sewage or gray water on the floor is a health problem as well as a mess. Our 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends, and we book same-day appointments when we can. Until a plumber arrives, the goal is to slow the flow and keep people out of the wet area.

Do not keep flushing to see if it clears. Every flush and every load of laundry adds a few more gallons to a line that has nowhere to send them.

  1. Stop using every sink, toilet, tub, and the washing machine.
  2. Keep children, pets, and anyone with a weak immune system away from the wet floor.
  3. Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords, or a furnace sit nearby.
  4. Move rugs, boxes, and stored items up off the floor.
  5. Note which fixtures are affected, because that tells the plumber where to start.
  6. Skip liquid drain openers. They can sit in the pipe and burn skin or eyes when the cable goes in.

One fixture or the whole house? How we sort it out

The first question on every call is how many drains are affected. One slow sink means a branch line problem, usually within 10 to 15 feet of the fixture. A tub, a toilet, and a floor drain all acting up together point to the main drain that leaves the house.

That split changes the tool, the time, and the cost. Branch clogs respond to a small cable. Main drain blockages often need a larger cable head or a camera, and our sewer line cleaning service covers the deeper run toward the street.

If the same line clogs again every few months, a cable only opens a hole through the buildup. Scraping the full pipe wall with hydro jetting is the better fix for that pattern.

How a drain clearing visit runs

  1. Talk through the pattern

    We ask which fixtures are slow, when it started, and what has gone down the drain. Wipes, grease, and hair each leave a different kind of blockage.

  2. Find the access point

    The cable goes in through a cleanout, a removed trap, or a pulled toilet. A cleanout near the foundation is the easiest way into a main line.

  3. Cut through the clog

    A motorized cable with a cutting head spins through the blockage and pulls debris back out. Different heads handle soft sludge, hair, and root fibers.

  4. Run water and test

    We run the fixture at full flow for several minutes. A drain that only looks open can still choke when a full tub empties.

  5. Camera the pipe when needed

    If the clog returns or the cable stops short, a small camera shows whether the pipe is cracked, sagging, or packed with scale.

What a technician actually checks on a drain call

The cable is the visible part of the job. The checks around it decide whether the fix lasts.

  • ✓Which fixtures drain normally, which drain slowly, and which are stopped.
  • ✓Whether a cleanout exists, and where it sits on the main line.
  • ✓How far the cable travels before it meets resistance, measured in feet.
  • ✓What the cable brings back, such as hair, grease, roots, or paper.
  • ✓Whether water runs freely at full flow after the clog opens.
  • ✓Whether a vent stack on the roof is blocked, if gurgling continues.
  • ✓Whether a camera view is needed to confirm the pipe wall is sound.

Why kitchen lines clog differently than bathroom lines

Hot grease runs freely, then cools and hardens on the inside of the pipe. Layer after layer narrows a 1.5 or 2-inch drain until a single bit of food catches. A garbage disposal grinds scraps, but it does nothing about the grease coating the pipe beyond it. If your disposal is part of the problem, our garbage disposal repair page covers that side.

Mineral film and older lines in Independence homes

What changes the price of a drain cleaning

Two clogged drains can look the same from the sink and cost very different amounts to open. The price follows the pipe, the access, and the cause, so we quote after we see which situation you have.

Distance to the clog

A blockage 8 feet from a sink is a short job. One 80 feet down the main takes more cable, more time, and a bigger machine.

Access to the line

A cleanout makes entry simple. Pulling a toilet or cutting into a trap adds labor.

What the clog is made of

Soft sludge opens fast. Packed roots, scale, or a foreign object can take several passes with different heads.

Need for a camera or jetting

A recurring clog may need a camera view or a full jetting pass, which is a separate step from the first opening.

After-hours scheduling

Night, weekend, and holiday calls follow different scheduling than a weekday appointment, and we tell you before we start.
