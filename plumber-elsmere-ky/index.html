The plumbing problems that older inner-ring houses keep running into

Most Elsmere calls fall into a handful of patterns. Knowing which one you have makes the first phone call much shorter.

Main drain that backs up

Roots, scale, and bellied sections of old pipe slow the flow until one bad night stops it. Several fixtures acting up together points to the main.

Cracked or collapsed sewer pipe

Clay and cast iron lines from earlier building eras can split at joints. A camera shows whether a repair or a replacement section is the right call.

Corroded supply pipes

Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, so water pressure drops long before anything leaks. Brown water at first use is a sign.

Failing water heater

A tank that rumbles, leaks at the base, or runs out of hot water fast is near the end. We can usually tell on arrival whether a repair makes sense.

What settling in 1885 means for the pipes under your floor

South Erlanger began as a settlement in 1885, and the town took the name Elsmere after Elsmere Avenue in Norwood, Ohio, the hometown of one of its founders. For a plumber, the name matters less than the date. Homes from that era and the decades after often have layers of repair.

A house like that may have original cast iron drain pipe, newer PVC patches, and copper or galvanized supply lines in the same wall. Each material wears differently, and joining them takes care. We do not assume. A technician checks the transitions first because that is where leaks and blockages collect.

If your home has been remodeled, tell us where. A bathroom added in a later decade may drain into the old line at an awkward angle, and that is a common spot for repeat clogs.

Sewage or water coming up inside an Elsmere house

When water or sewage is entering the house, speed matters, but safety comes first. Sewage carries bacteria, so avoid touching it with bare hands, and never run a vacuum on wet floors.

Call our 24/7 line before you start cleaning. We can tell you what to do first based on where the water is rising.

  1. Stop using every sink, toilet, tub, washer, and dishwasher so no more water enters the line.
  2. Shut off the main water valve if a supply pipe is the source.
  3. Cut the breaker for any room with wet floors, provided you can get to the panel without crossing water.
  4. Keep children and pets out of the affected room and away from standing water.
  5. Open windows for air, then call the 24/7 line and describe what is coming up and from which drain.
