The sewer line is the single large pipe, usually 4 inches across, that carries every drain in the house toward the street. When it narrows, the lowest fixture in the house tells you first. In many Kenton County homes that is a basement floor drain or a ground-floor tub.

Six warning signs your main sewer line is closing up

The main line gives early hints. Catching them before sewage reaches the floor changes a cleanup into a simple service call.

Toilet bubbles when water drains elsewhere

Air trapped behind a partial blockage escapes through the nearest water seal. A bubble after a shower or laundry load is a strong hint.

Several fixtures slow at once

One slow drain is local. A toilet, a tub, and a sink all dragging means the shared line is restricted.

Basement floor drain gurgles or weeps

It sits lowest in the house, so backed-up water shows there before anywhere else.

Sewer odor inside or in the yard

A damp, rotten smell near the foundation or a drain points to waste sitting in the pipe.

Green, lush patches over the line

Leaking waste feeds grass. A strip of unusually thick lawn between house and street can mark a crack or open joint.

Repeat clogs after cable service

If a main line clogs again within weeks, roots or a damaged section are likely the reason.

Sewage is coming up into the house

Raw sewage carries bacteria, so treat any backup as a contamination issue. Our 24/7 line takes calls at night and on weekends, and we book same-day appointments when the schedule allows. While you wait, protect the people and rooms around the backup.

  1. Stop using every toilet, sink, tub, and the washing machine.
  2. Keep children and pets away from standing water.
  3. Do not run a wet vacuum on sewage or flip switches in a wet area.
  4. Open a window if the odor is strong, and wear gloves if you must touch anything wet.
  5. Move furniture, boxes, and rugs away from the spill.

How a sewer line cleaning visit runs

  1. Locate the cleanout

    A capped pipe near the foundation, in a basement, or in the yard gives direct access to the main. Without one, we pull a toilet or work from a roof vent.

  2. Run the cable to the stopping point

    A large drum cable with a root-cutting or spear head travels toward the street. We note the distance in feet when it meets resistance.

  3. Clear and flush

    Once the blockage breaks apart, we flush the line with water to carry debris downstream and confirm full flow.

  4. Camera the line

    A camera pass shows whether the pipe is sound or needs further work. You see the same footage we do.

What a technician actually checks on a sewer line call

  • ✓Which fixtures are affected and which still drain.
  • ✓Whether neighbors have similar backups, which could point to the public main.
  • ✓The location and condition of the cleanout cap.
  • ✓What the cable brings back: roots, paper, wipes, grease, or scale.
  • ✓The distance to the blockage, and whether it matches a joint or a bend.
  • ✓The camera view of pipe walls after the line is open.

How tree roots find a sewer joint

Roots follow moisture. A joint that seeps a little water gives a root hair a place to enter, and the root grows thicker inside the pipe over seasons. A cable can cut it back, but the root remains attached outside, so the line often needs cleaning again. If the camera finds roots at a joint, read about our sewer line repair options.

What shapes the price of cleaning a sewer line

A main line job varies more than a sink job because the pipe is longer, deeper, and sometimes harder to reach. These factors set the number.

Cleanout access

A working cleanout speeds the job. Pulling a toilet or working from a roof vent adds labor.

Length and depth of the line

A short run to the street is simpler than a long run with bends.

Cable or jetting

Soft blockages open with a cable, while heavy scale or grease may call for hydro jetting instead.

Camera inspection

Footage before and after adds time but reveals damage the cable cannot see.

Time of the call

Night and weekend work is scheduled differently from a weekday visit, and we explain that up front.

Insurance often covers sudden backup damage when you carry water backup coverage, but rarely covers clearing a clog that built up slowly. Read your own policy to see what applies.
