Plumbing installation in Independence, KY covers the work that adds something new to a house: a water softener, a gas line, a tub or shower, a washer hookup, or the rough-in pipes in a new build. Our licensed and insured plumbers plan the route, make the connections, and test the result. You get a clear price before work starts, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

Installs go wrong when the plan skips a detail, such as a vent that was never added or a pipe too small for the load. That is why this page starts with the scope of work and what each type of install involves.

Signs your house needs new plumbing, not another patch

Some problems come from the plumbing that was never built for how you live now. These are the clues.

No plumbing at all in a new space

A basement bath, garage sink, or finished addition needs supply, drain, and vent pipes brought in.

Old S-traps under sinks

These can siphon dry and let sewer smell in, and a modern P-trap with a proper vent fixes that.

A tub or shower you want upgraded

A new valve, drain, and supply lines are installed together so the whole stack works as one.

A stove or grill moved to gas

A new appliance needs the right pipe size, a shutoff, and a leak check before it is used.

Hard water scale on fixtures

White crust on faucets and spotty glass often point to a softener or filter.

A remodel with open walls

This is the cheapest time to move pipes, since nothing has to be cut open later.

Water softener and filtration installs

A softener swaps the calcium and magnesium that cause scale for sodium or potassium, and a filter removes taste, odor, or particles. Both need a drain for backwash, a power outlet nearby, and bypass valves so you can isolate the unit. We also service and reconnect existing systems, including units that were damaged in a move or a construction project.

NKWD's 2023 report shows about 7 grains per gallon of hardness across its systems, which sits in the moderately hard to hard range. The report does not say which plant serves which street, so test your own tap before choosing a size. Buying a larger tank than you need wastes salt, and a smaller one regenerates too often.

Chlorine is the disinfectant NKWD lists, and a carbon filter on the kitchen tap or the whole house reduces its taste. Under-sink reverse osmosis is a separate option for drinking water at one faucet. If you have already bought the equipment, bring the model number and we will plan the hookup around it.

Gas line work starts with safety, not tools

If you smell gas, leave the building right away and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches, light a flame, or use your phone indoors. Call a plumber only after the utility says the area is safe. We do not promise a response time on gas odor calls, because the utility handles that first.

For planned work, such as a stove, a grill hookup, or a line to a fireplace, the pipe is sized to the appliance, fitted with a shutoff, and tested for leaks before it is put to use. A rusty or old line can be inspected before you rely on it.

New construction rough-in plumbing

Rough-in is the first stage of plumbing in a new build. Drain, waste, and vent pipes go in before the walls close, and supply lines are run to each fixture location. Everything is pressure tested so a leak is found while it is easy to reach.

This stage depends on the plans and on whoever builds the framing, so we work with your general contractor on timing. Permit rules vary, so ask the Kentucky Division of Plumbing or the city before work begins. A plumber can explain what to ask for on your project.

What a technician checks before installing anything

A good install is decided before the first pipe is cut. These checks come first. If the fixture you are replacing is just a leaky tap, faucet repair may be all you need.

  • ✓Measure the water pressure at the spot where the new fixture will go.
  • ✓Check that the drain size and slope can carry the new load.
  • ✓Find the nearest vent, or plan a new one if the fixture needs it.
  • ✓Confirm shutoff valves are present and that they actually close.
  • ✓Look for a clear path for pipe through framing, crawl space, or slab.
  • ✓Check that the fixture or appliance matches the supply and fuel type.
  • ✓Decide where the cutoffs and test points go for later service.

How an install visit usually goes

  1. Walk the site

    The technician looks at the space, the existing pipes, and the equipment you bought.

  2. Lay out the plan

    We mark pipe routes, valve locations, and any cuts needed, and tell you what each step will cost.

  3. Rough in and connect

    Supply, drain, vent, or gas pipe is run, with fittings suited to the material.

  4. Test under load

    Water lines are pressure tested, drains are flooded, and gas connections are checked for leaks.

  5. Set and finish

    Fixtures are mounted and sealed, appliances are started, and the area is cleaned.

What affects the price of an installation

Installation prices swing more than repair prices, because the scope can range from one valve to a full build. A technician needs to see the space first.

Distance to existing pipes

A fixture next to a supply and drain is simple, while one across the house means more pipe and more labor.

Type of equipment

A basic softener, a multistage filter, and a gas range hookup each need different parts and fittings.

Access

Open framing is quick work. Finished walls, concrete floors, and tight crawl spaces add time.

Venting and drain changes

A fixture with no vent nearby may need new vent piping routed through the wall or attic.

Scope and phasing

New construction priced by the stage is different from a single remodel visit, so scope is spelled out up front.

Installations of new equipment are usually not covered by insurance, since they are planned upgrades rather than sudden damage. If a failed install later causes a leak, read your own policy to see how it responds.
