A burst pipe moves fast. A split in a half-inch line can release several gallons a minute, and water will find the ceiling below, the wall cavity, and the electrical outlets. Water is spraying or pouring from a pipe right now

Stopping the water comes before anything else. The sooner the pressure is off the line, the less drywall, flooring, and insulation soak up. Our dispatcher can talk you through the steps while a plumber is on the way, and after-hours calls get the same priority as daytime ones.

  1. Find the main water valve, usually near the water heater or where the line enters the house, and turn it fully off.
  2. If the valve will not turn, look for the shutoff near the meter and tell us. A break before the meter belongs to the water district, so call its customer service line too.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to let trapped water drain out.
  4. Turn off the breaker for any room with water on the floor or ceiling.
  5. Shut off the water heater so its elements or burner do not run dry.
  6. Move rugs and furniture, and mop up standing water.

Cracked elbows, split copper and failed joints: what a burst pipe can look like

Callers use many words for the same trouble: a split, a crack, a blowout, a pipe that let go. Here is what each looks like and what usually caused it.

Split seam in a straight run

A long slit along a copper or galvanized pipe often points to frozen water that expanded inside it.

Cracked elbow or fitting

Elbows take stress from pressure surges and movement. A hairline crack there can open under load and spray.

Failed solder or glued joint

A joint can let go where the solder is old or a glued plastic joint was never fully seated.

Pinhole spray

Corrosion can wear a tiny hole in a thin spot, and the water shoots out as a fine, hard stream.

Brittle plastic pipe

CPVC and older PVC can turn brittle and snap, sometimes with little warning.

Broken galvanized steel

Rust eats the inside wall of a galvanized pipe until a section gives way under pressure.

How we repair a split or broken line

  1. Confirm the water is off

    We check the shutoff and drain the line so the cut is dry.

  2. Find the full extent of the damage

    A frozen pipe may have split in more than one place. We look along the run and test the neighboring pipe.

  3. Cut out the bad section

    We remove the damaged length back to sound pipe on both sides.

  4. Install new pipe and fittings

    We use sleeves, couplings, or a soldered, crimped, or glued section that matches the existing material.

  5. Restore pressure and test

    We turn the water back on slowly, check every joint for seep, and bleed air from the lines.

Pipe material and the usual repair

The fix depends on what the pipe is made of. This is how common materials in homes behave when they fail.

Pipe typeHow it usually failsTypical repair
CopperFreeze split, pinhole, or failed jointCut out the section and sweat in a new piece or use a coupling
Galvanized steelRust-thinned wall gives wayReplace the run, since neighboring pipe is also worn
CPVCBrittle crack at a fitting or bendReplace the section and check the nearby fittings
PVCCrack from impact or frostCut and glue a new piece with couplings
PEXFitting leak or freeze bulgeReplace the fitting or the stretched section

Where the old pipe is worn along its whole length, a spot fix just moves the failure. We tell you when a longer section needs to be replaced.

Smaller leaks that still need a plumber soon

Not every call is a gusher. A drip at a copper joint, a mist from a half-inch line, or a damp spot on a ceiling are early versions of a burst. Fast action keeps the repair small.

A pin-sized hole in copper can be sleeved. A failed solder joint can be re-soldered once the pipe is dry and cleaned. A stain that keeps growing means water is still moving, so shut off the line to that room until we arrive. If you cannot tell where it starts, our leak detection team can trace it.

Why a frozen pipe splits after it thaws

Water expands about nine percent when it freezes. The ice plug holds the pressure, and the pipe wall stretches or cracks nearby. You often do not see the leak until the ice melts and water flows through the opening. If you find a frozen pipe, keep the water off and call before you thaw it.

What a technician checks before cutting

Before we cut a pipe, we want to know the full story behind the failure.

  • ✓Confirm the water is fully off and the line is drained.
  • ✓Trace the pipe in both directions to find every damaged spot.
  • ✓Identify the pipe material and whether it matches what is in the wall.
  • ✓Look for a freeze, corrosion, or a pressure problem behind the failure.
  • ✓Check the water pressure at a nearby fixture after the repair.
  • ✓Check the wall, floor, and ceiling below for hidden wetness.
  • ✓Test the new joints under full pressure before closing anything up.

Drain pipes, sewer lines and repeat failures

A cracked drain or sewer pipe is a different problem from a supply burst. A drain crack leaks wastewater, often below a toilet, in a crawlspace, or behind a wall. A localized crack in a plastic sewer line, sometimes with roots pushing in, can often be fixed by replacing that section.

If several drains back up at once, the cause may be the public sewer, and Sanitation District No. 1 takes sewer emergency reports. A plumber can first check whether the blockage sits in your own pipes.

If a home has had one burst pipe, another is possible nearby, especially with old galvanized lines or brittle plastic pipe. Ask about repiping when more than one section has failed. If the failed pipe is the main line into the house, read about water line repair and replacement.

What decides the cost of a pipe repair

A small accessible repair and a failure behind a finished wall are very different jobs. Here is what moves the price.

How easy the pipe is to reach

An exposed basement pipe is quick. A pipe inside a finished wall or under a floor takes cutting and patching.

Pipe material and size

Copper costs more to work with than plastic. Larger lines need larger fittings.

Length of damaged pipe

A single crack is a short repair. A freeze that split several spots takes more pipe and time.

Water damage and cleanup

Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring may need to be removed so they can dry.

Timing of the call

A same-day or after-hours visit can price differently from a scheduled daytime slot. You see the price first.

Homeowners insurance often covers sudden water damage from a burst pipe, including cleanup and repairs to what the water ruined. It rarely pays to replace a pipe that wore out slowly, so read your policy and call your agent promptly.
