How to Install a Garbage Disposal in Independence, KY

A swap takes a few hours with basic tools, and the dishwasher knockout is the step most people forget.

By Independence Pro PlumbingPublished October 15, 2026Updated October 15, 2026
Call (855) 575-2890

Installing a garbage disposal in Independence, KY comes down to four jobs: mount the unit under the sink flange, open the dishwasher inlet if you have a dishwasher, connect power, and hook up the drain. Replacing an old disposal with a similar one is a manageable afternoon project. A first-time install where no outlet or switch exists is a bigger job.

Below is the order of work, the parts you will handle, the mistakes that cause leaks and jams, and the points where hiring a licensed plumber or electrician is the smarter move.

Gather these parts and tools first

Having everything on the counter before you start prevents the classic mid-job trip to the hardware store with the sink in pieces.

  • ✓A disposal sized to the household, commonly a half horsepower to one horsepower motor.
  • ✓The mounting kit that comes in the box: sink flange, fiber gasket, backup ring, mounting ring and snap ring.
  • ✓Plumber's putty, a flat screwdriver, a hammer and a pair of channel-lock pliers.
  • ✓A new discharge gasket and a drain elbow, plus slip-joint washers for the trap.
  • ✓A power cord kit if the unit has no plug, or wire connectors rated for the cable.
  • ✓A bucket, towels and a flashlight for the dark cabinet.
  • ✓A hacksaw or tubing cutter, since the trap often needs trimming.

Order of work from old unit to new

  1. Cut power and clear the cabinet

    Switch off the breaker that feeds the disposal and unplug or disconnect it. Empty the cabinet and place a bucket under the trap.

  2. Remove the old unit

    Twist the mounting ring with a screwdriver in the lug to release it, support the body, and lower it down. Disconnect the dishwasher hose and the discharge pipe first.

  3. Build the new sink mount

    Press a rope of plumber's putty under the new flange lip and seat it in the sink opening. From below, add the gasket, backup ring and mounting ring, then seat the snap ring until it clicks.

  4. Tighten the mounting screws evenly

    Turn each of the three screws a little at a time so the flange pulls down flat. Wipe off the squeezed-out putty from the sink surface.

  5. Prepare the body

    Open the electrical cover plate, attach the cord or cable, and knock out the dishwasher plug if needed. Then lift the unit onto the mounting ring and twist until the lugs lock.

  6. Connect the drain and test

    Attach the discharge elbow with its gasket, line up the trap, and tighten the nuts by hand plus a quarter turn. Run water for two minutes and check every joint with a dry paper towel.

The dishwasher knockout nobody wants to miss

Most disposals ship with a thin plastic plug sealing the dishwasher inlet, a short side stub near the top of the housing. If you have a dishwasher, that plug has to come out. If you do not, leave it in place.

Set a flat screwdriver on the plug, tap with a hammer until it breaks free, and fish the plug out of the grinding chamber with pliers. Forget that last part and the plug can jam the grinder the first time it runs. Then attach the dishwasher drain hose with a clamp, and follow the dishwasher maker's guidance for routing the hose so wastewater does not flow back.

Hardwired or plug-in: which setup fits your sink

The electrical side is where homeowners most often hit their limits. Choose the setup based on what the cabinet already has.

Most units run on standard 120 volt household power. Black connects to black, white to white, and the bare or green wire to the green ground screw. Test the circuit with a voltage tester before you touch a wire.

Check the license and ask about permits near Independence

Local note

Plumbing in Kentucky is overseen by the Division of Plumbing, part of the state Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. You can verify any plumber's license with the Kentucky Division of Plumbing before you hire them, and that is worth a quick check if you are paying someone to run a new drain or supply line.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Kentucky Division of Plumbing or the city before you start a project that changes pipework. A swap of one disposal for another is a lighter job than a new drain run, but a short phone call gets you a clear answer before you buy parts.

Why leaks start at the flange, not the drain

Most leaks after an install come from the top of the sink, not the pipes. If the putty bead is thin, or the mounting screws are tightened unevenly, water seeps between the flange and the sink, runs down the outside of the unit, and drips off the bottom where it looks like a body leak. Trace any drip with a dry paper towel from the flange down.

Mistakes that cause jams and smells

The first mistake is skipping the putty and relying on the gasket alone. The second is over-tightening the trap nuts, which cracks plastic fittings. The third is feeding the new unit the wrong food. Fibrous items like celery and corn husks wrap around the grinding plate, and grease hardens in the drain beyond it.

Run cold water while the unit grinds and for 15 seconds afterward. Cold water keeps fats solid so they flush out in pieces. Avoid pouring cooking oil down the drain, and never put your hand into the chamber. Use the hex socket on the underside to free a jam after power is off.

When to hire a plumber instead

Call a licensed plumber if the drain is slow before you install, if the trap does not line up with the new outlet, or if the cabinet has water damage. A new disposal will not fix a clogged branch line, and a bad trap setup can leak even with a brand-new unit.
