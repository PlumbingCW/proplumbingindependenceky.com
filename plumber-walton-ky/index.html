Before you pick up the phone, figure out which side of the meter the problem is on. That one question decides who fixes it, and it matters more in Walton than in many towns, because the city runs its own water and sewer utility. For the pipes on your side, an emergency plumber Walton, KY homeowners can call is Independence Pro Plumbing, working from our office about 6 miles to the northeast.

The city handles the main in the street and the equipment that serves it. You handle the line from the meter or tap into your house, the plumbing inside, and the drain that leaves the building. We cover that property side, around the clock.

What follows explains how to tell the two apart, which numbers to call for each, and what we do once we are on your property.

Where the city's pipe ends and yours begins

A city utility owns and repairs the water main under the street and the sewer main it connects to. A homeowner owns the service pipe from the meter to the house, the fixtures, and the drain line that runs out to the city's sewer. The exact point where responsibility switches can vary, so ask the city where its responsibility ends on your street.

The easiest clue is the water meter. If water is bubbling up in the street or the yard in front of the meter, the problem is probably the city's. If water is wet on the house side, in the basement, or under the slab, it is probably yours. City Hall, at 40 North Main Street, is the place to ask, and the after-hours line is the one to use for a main break.

Here is a quick test. Turn off every faucet and check whether the meter dial still moves. If it does, water is escaping somewhere past the meter, and that is our side of the line.

Why a city utility changes the order of your phone calls

Walton is in Boone County, and the city extends slightly into southwestern Kenton County. It was established in 1840, and the railroad reached it in 1869. Today about 5,460 people live in the city, and I-75 exit 171 puts KY 14 and KY 16 within easy reach.

For a homeowner, the city-run utility means your starting-service paperwork, bills, and main-break reports all go through the city. Opening an account takes a deposit, and the same office is the place to report a main break. We do not take that role. Our job starts at your property line, which is why we ask you to confirm who owns the leak first.
