Drains that gurgle, back up or smell: when to look inside the line

Sewer trouble starts quietly. The signs below often show up weeks or months before a full backup, and they usually point to the same underground pipe.

Several drains slow at once

One slow sink is local. Slow tubs, toilets and a floor drain together suggest the main line.

Gurgling after a flush

Air pushed back through a trap can mean a partial blockage downstream.

Sewage smell in the yard or basement

A cracked or offset joint can leak gas and wastewater into the soil.

Lush or soggy patches over the line

A damp, extra-green strip of lawn can mark a leak, since sewage feeds the grass.

Repeat clogs after cleaning

If a cleaning only buys a few weeks, something in the pipe, like roots or a belly, keeps catching waste.

Sinkholes or sunken ground

Soil washing into a broken pipe leaves voids that collapse over time.

How a camera inspection works, one step at a time

  1. Find the access point

    We start at a cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the house or in the basement. If none exists, we can pull a toilet or work from a roof vent.

  2. Protect the area

    We lay down floor covers, wear gloves and set up the monitor so you can watch.

  3. Run the camera

    A self-leveling camera head is pushed through the 4 or 6 inch pipe while a transmitter reports the depth and distance.

  4. Mark and record

    Each defect is logged by footage count, and we can mark the ground above it with paint or a flag.

  5. Review the video

    We show you the footage on site and explain which findings need work now and which can wait.

  6. Choose next steps

    You may need nothing, a cleaning, or a repair. We give a clear price before work starts.

What a technician looks for in the footage

The camera tells a story only if someone reads it carefully. We check these items on every run.

  • ✓Pipe material: PVC, cast iron, clay tile or Orangeburg fiber pipe.
  • ✓Joint condition and any gaps or offsets.
  • ✓Root intrusion, from hairlines to thick masses.
  • ✓Sags or bellies that hold water after waste passes.
  • ✓Cracks, breaks or collapsed sections.
  • ✓Buildup of grease, scale or debris on the pipe wall.
  • ✓Connection points where branch lines meet the main.
  • ✓The total length run and the depth at key points.

A clean video is not a perfect pipe

A camera sees the inside surface, not what is happening in the soil. A pipe can look fine today and still have a bellied section that only fills after heavy use. We note slope, water lines and joint spacing so you know where to watch. For a deeper check, repeat the inspection after a major repair or before a house sale.

What drives the cost of a sewer camera inspection

Inspections look similar, but the work behind them differs. We give a clear price before work starts, after we see your access point.

Access to the line

A clear cleanout makes the job quick. Pulling a toilet or working from a roof vent takes more time.

Length and depth of the run

A long lateral to the street main needs more cable and more time than a short run.

Pre-cleaning

Heavy grease or roots may need to be cleared before the camera can see the wall.

Locating and marking

Pinpointing and flagging a defect above ground adds a locating step.

Written report

Buyers and sellers often want video files and a summary to share.

Insurance may help when a sudden sewer backup damages the home, and some policies need a separate backup endorsement. Wear-out repairs to the pipe itself are rarely covered, so read your own policy.

Whose pipe is it? Sorting private lines from the district main

Local note

Sanitation District No. 1 handles sewer and stormwater service in the area, and it has a Report a Sewer Emergency option on its website. A camera run shows whether the blockage is in your private line or farther out. If the trouble sits in the street main, you need the district, and the footage gives you something to show them.

What homes of different ages tend to have underground

Independence grew through annexation in the 1960s, the 1970s and 1981, so homes here come from several building eras. Older homes may have clay or cast iron lines that tend to crack or corrode, while newer ones often use PVC. These are general trends, not facts about any one street. That is why a camera, not a guess, tells you what your house has.

If footage shows damage, sewer line repair is the next page to read. If it shows buildup, sewer line cleaning usually handles it. We also look after homeowners in Edgewood.
