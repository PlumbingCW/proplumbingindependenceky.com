A mill, a creek, and the road that still carries our trucks

The city takes its name from a mill on Bank Lick Creek. James Taylor Jr., who founded Newport, bought the land in 1810, and the mill ran in the early 1800s. The route from what is now Covington to that mill became Taylor's Mill Road, the same road we use today.

Why does that matter to you at midnight? Because we are not guessing at a route. Our office is on the same road, about 5 miles away in a straight line, and we dispatch by the most direct way. We never promise an arrival time, but we will tell you when a technician is actually on the way.

Burst pipe or active leak in Taylor Mill: what to do before we arrive

Water damage grows by the minute, and drywall soaks it up quickly. Stopping the flow is the most useful thing you can do before a technician gets there.

If you cannot find the shutoff or the water is near outlets, leave the area and call our 24/7 line right away. We can talk you through it.

  1. Turn off the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Cut the breakers for any wet room, but only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  3. Move people and pets out of any room with standing water near outlets or appliances.
  4. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the pipes and relieve pressure.
  5. Call our 24/7 line and describe where the water is coming from and what you have shut off.

Water supply basics for Taylor Mill homeowners

Northern Kentucky Water District runs a treatment plant named Taylor Mill, which draws from the Licking River. That is only the plant's name. The district's 2023 report shows about 7 grains per gallon of hardness across its systems, which counts as moderately hard to hard water.

That hardness is the real reason water heaters here wear out. Minerals settle at the bottom of a tank and force the burner or element to work harder. Flushing a tank once a year slows the build-up, and a technician can tell you if it is already too late.

The district owns the service line from the main to your meter, and you own the rest from the meter to the house. If the break is before the meter, the district should be called. If it is after, that is our job. A technician can help you tell which side you are on.
