Plumbing in Independence: what the city and the water tell us

Independence was first called Crewitts Creek in 1837, became a town in 1842 around the Kenton County Courthouse on Madison Pike, and now covers more than 23 square miles. The city grew by annexation, adding the Cherokee section in the 1960s, the Beechgrove area in the early 1970s and land west to the Boone County line in 1981.

That means homes of very different ages sit side by side. The first question we ask about any leak is how old the house is and what the pipes are made of.

The Northern Kentucky Water District reports about 7 grains per gallon of hardness across its systems in its 2023 water quality report, which is moderately hard to hard. Hard water leaves scale inside water heaters and on fixtures over the years. That is why water heater repair calls so often end with a conversation about sediment and flushing.

The district owns the service line from the main to the meter, and the property owner owns the rest, from the meter to the house. If the leak is in the yard on your side, water line repair is a job for a plumber. If you are unsure, call us and we will help you work out which side of the meter it is on.

Sewer and stormwater in the area are handled by Sanitation District No. 1. A backup that starts inside the house is usually a problem in your own line, so sewer line cleaning and a camera look are the usual first steps. Low spots near Banklick Creek and other drainage ways can collect runoff after heavy rain, which is why sump pump repair is part of our work too.
