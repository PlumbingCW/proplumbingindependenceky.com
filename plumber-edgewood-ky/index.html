Four seasons, four kinds of plumbing calls

Winter: frozen and split lines

Pipes in exterior walls, garages, and crawlspaces can freeze and split. Water often does not pour out until the ice melts, so a quiet pipe is not a safe pipe.

Spring: wet basements and sump pumps

Heavy rain tests every sump pit. A pump that is stuck, unplugged, or worn out leaves the basement to flood while you sleep.

Summer: heavy hot-water demand

Houseguests, laundry, and long showers push tanks hard. Failing elements and leaking tank bases tend to show up now.

Fall: shutoffs and hose bibs

Disconnect hoses and drain outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze. A forgotten hose can split a faucet stem inside the wall.

Why a quiet frozen pipe is still an emergency

Ice expands about nine percent as it forms, and that force can split copper, PEX, or plastic. The split often sits behind a wall or above a ceiling, hidden until the ice melts and water floods out. By then the damage may be spreading quietly for hours.

If a faucet gives only a trickle on a cold morning, shut off the main and call before you try to thaw anything. A heat gun near a plastic pipe can do more harm than the freeze did. A technician can warm the pipe slowly and check for splits before the water is turned back on.

A frozen or burst pipe in your Edgewood home right now

Treat a split pipe like a flood waiting to happen. Even a small crack can release gallons over an hour, and cold weather keeps people from noticing. Shut off the water first, then deal with the mess.

A gas smell changes everything: get outside first and phone the gas utility or 911, then call us.

  1. Close the main water valve completely, then open the nearest faucet to relieve pressure.
  2. Shut off the breakers feeding the soaked rooms if you can reach the panel on dry footing.
  3. Move rugs, boxes, and electronics away from the wet area and up off the floor.
  4. Do not use a flame or torch to thaw a frozen pipe.
  5. Call our 24/7 line and tell dispatch where the frozen or split pipe is.
