The tank gave out suddenly: your first ten minutes

A failed tank can release the whole 40 to 50 gallons within minutes, and the water goes wherever the floor slopes. We answer after-hours calls for flooding heaters, and a replacement can often be scheduled the same day.

Protect people first, then property. Stay out of standing water near electrical panels or outlets.

  1. Turn the cold water valve above the tank fully off.
  2. Cut power at the breaker, or switch a gas heater's control to off.
  3. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.
  4. Open a hot faucet at the highest point in the house so the tank drains.
  5. Pull up rugs and move boxes off the wet floor.
  6. Call our 24/7 line with the tank's size, fuel type and where it sits in the home.

Which kind of new heater fits your house

The right replacement depends on fuel, space and how many people shower each morning.

Standard gas tank

Heats quickly and works with a vent and gas line already in place. Venting must be inspected with every swap.

Standard electric tank

Simple to install and has no combustion air to worry about. Recovery is slower than gas.

Larger tank

A 50 or 75 gallon model suits a busy household that ran out of hot water on the old 40 gallon tank.

Tankless unit

Heats on demand and frees floor space. It needs the right gas line or electrical circuit. See our tankless water heater page.

Heat pump or hybrid tank

An option that moves heat from the surrounding air and uses less electricity. It needs open space, a floor drain or condensate line, and often a larger footprint.

How hard water shapes the life of your next tank

Local note

Northern Kentucky Water District's 2023 report lists about 7 grains per gallon across its systems. That level lays limescale on heating surfaces, and the scale insulates the water from the burner or element. A new tank that is flushed yearly keeps its efficiency, while one left alone often wears out sooner.

So ask us about a drain valve you can reach, and plan a simple annual flush from day one.

Reading the data plate on your old tank

The sticker on the side lists capacity in gallons, the first-hour rating, fuel type and the manufacture date in the serial number. Those four facts tell us what to match, and whether the tank has outlived the typical lifespan of roughly ten to fifteen years for a standard unit.

Take a photo of it before we arrive. It speeds up the order for the right replacement, especially after hours.

Planning for a tank that has not failed yet

The cheapest replacement is the one you schedule. A planned swap lets you pick the size, compare fuel options and clear the area around the heater. It also avoids the damage that follows a split tank, such as wet drywall and ruined stored items.

Warning signs include rust-tinted hot water, moisture at the base, a tank that rumbles, and a water heater that cycles on and off more than it used to. If you notice two of these together, book a visit while the floor is still dry.

How a replacement visit runs from start to finish

  1. Confirm the match

    We read the old tank's data plate for fuel, capacity and recovery rate, then compare it with your household use.

  2. Drain and disconnect

    The tank is isolated, drained through a hose and disconnected from water, gas or power, and the vent.

  3. Set the new unit

    The new heater goes on a level base with a drain pan where the space allows, and new connectors replace worn ones.

  4. Reconnect and test

    We check gas joints with a leak solution or meter, test the relief valve and verify vent draft on gas units.

  5. Fill, heat and haul away

    The tank fills before power returns, we confirm the water reaches temperature, and we remove the old heater.

What gets inspected beyond the tank itself

A swap is a chance to catch problems that shortened the last tank's life.

  • ✓Condition of the shutoff valves and whether they close fully.
  • ✓Flexible connectors and copper stubs for corrosion or kinks.
  • ✓Gas line size, sediment trap and flue pipe slope on gas units.
  • ✓Breaker size and wire gauge on electric units.
  • ✓Temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe.
  • ✓Expansion tank presence where a closed system calls for one.
  • ✓Water pressure at a hose bib, since high pressure shortens tank life.

What moves the price of a new water heater job

Two homes with the same 40 gallon tank can get very different totals. We explain every line before the first wrench turns.

Tank type and capacity

Gas, electric, larger sizes, tankless and heat pump models sit in different price tiers.

Venting and gas work

A flue that needs rerouting or a new gas line adds parts and hours.

Code-related upgrades

Older installations sometimes need new valves, a pan, or an expansion tank added during the swap.

Access and removal

Stairs, tight closets and attic spaces take more labor to haul a full tank out.

Timing

Same-day and after-hours replacements are priced for the urgency, and you see the number before we begin.
