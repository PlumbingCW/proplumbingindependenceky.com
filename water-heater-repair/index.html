Cold showers, strange noises, or a puddle: what your tank is telling you

Homeowners usually describe the trouble before they know the cause. Here are the common signs and what they point to inside a standard 40 to 50 gallon tank.

Water never gets hot

On an electric tank this often means a failed heating element or a tripped high-limit switch. On a gas tank, look for a pilot that will not stay lit.

Hot water runs out in minutes

A broken dip tube lets cold water mix with the hot at the top. Sediment piled on the bottom also cuts the usable volume.

Rumbling or popping

Steam bubbles are escaping from under a layer of hardened sediment. The tank is working harder than it should.

Rusty or brown hot water

If only the hot side is discolored, the tank lining or anode rod may be corroding. If both sides run brown, the cause is elsewhere in the plumbing.

Water around the base

A leak can come from a loose fitting, the relief valve, or the tank itself. Only the first two are repairable.

Rotten egg smell

Bacteria in the tank can react with the anode rod and produce sulfur odor in the hot water. Gas smell is different and means leave the house.

Water pooling around the heater or no heat at all: what to do before we arrive

A leaking tank can drop hundreds of gallons across a basement or garage floor, and wet drywall starts to swell within hours. We take after-hours calls for tanks that are flooding, and same-day appointments are often possible for no-heat problems.

Move stored boxes and anything electrical away from the water. Do not step into standing water if the floor outlet or panel is nearby.

  1. Shut the cold supply valve above the tank by turning the handle fully clockwise or a quarter turn.
  2. Turn off power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater's control to off.
  3. Open one hot faucet upstairs to relieve pressure if the relief valve is dripping hard.
  4. Smell gas? Leave the house, then call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.
  5. Use towels and a wet vac on the floor, and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  6. Call our 24/7 line and tell us the tank's fuel type, size and where the water is coming from.

Hard water, sediment and the noisy tank

Local note

Northern Kentucky Water District's 2023 report shows about 7 grains per gallon of hardness across its systems. That is moderately hard to hard water, and it leaves limescale on heating surfaces. A tank that is never flushed collects a crust on the bottom, which makes gas burners run longer and burns out lower electric elements.

The practical fix is a yearly drain and flush. Put it on the calendar, because a crusted bottom both raises your energy use and shortens the life of an otherwise healthy tank.

What a technician tests before touching a part

A repair call is a diagnosis first. These are the checks that decide whether your heater is worth fixing.

  • ✓Confirm power or gas supply reaches the unit and the shutoff valves are fully open.
  • ✓Test heating elements with a meter for continuity and ground faults.
  • ✓Check thermostat settings and the reset button on the high-limit switch.
  • ✓Inspect the pilot, thermocouple and burner flame color on gas units.
  • ✓Look at the vent for backdrafting, soot or loose joints.
  • ✓Test the temperature and pressure relief valve and check the discharge pipe.
  • ✓Pull the anode rod when accessible and judge how much metal is left.
  • ✓Measure incoming water pressure, since high pressure strains every tank.

Why one water heater repair costs more than another

A thermostat swap and a gas control replacement are different jobs even though both are called repair. We give a clear price before work starts, after we see the unit.

The part that failed

Elements and thermostats are inexpensive parts. Gas control valves and some electronic boards cost much more.

Fuel type and tank size

Gas units have burners, vents and gas lines to inspect. Larger 50 and 75 gallon tanks take more time to drain.

Access to the heater

A tank in a tight closet or attic takes longer to service than one sitting in a garage.

Age and condition

A tank near the end of its life may need a second repair soon after the first, which changes the value of fixing it.

After-hours timing

Night, weekend and holiday visits can carry a different rate than a daytime appointment.

Homeowners insurance often covers sudden water damage from a burst or failed tank, but usually not the cost of replacing the worn-out heater itself. Read your own policy or ask your agent before you file.

The anode rod, a small part that decides how long the tank lasts

Inside every steel tank hangs a metal rod, usually magnesium or aluminum, that corrodes so the tank wall does not. Once the rod is eaten away, the steel starts to rust. A check every few years can show whether a rod needs replacing, which costs far less than a new tank.

Fix it or replace it: how we help you decide

A heater under about ten years old with a single failed part is usually worth repairing. Older tanks that leak from the shell, rust at the base, or need a second major part are usually better replaced. If that sounds like your tank, read about water heater replacement before you approve a repair.

Some homeowners prefer to skip the tank altogether and ask about tankless water heaters. If water is also showing up in places the heater cannot explain, a leak detection visit can trace it. We also help neighbors in Taylor Mill with the same problems.
