How we handle calls outside business hours

Plumbing problems at a business often start after closing, when nobody is watching the floor. Call our 24/7 line and describe what you see: where the water is, whether it is clean or foul, and whether you can reach the shutoff. Dispatch records the building details so the technician arrives knowing what to expect.

Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the after-hours line is open nights, weekends and holidays. If you manage more than one site, say so on the call. We note the shutoff locations and access instructions for each building so the next call goes faster.

Water is spreading through the building

A running leak in a commercial space can reach electrical panels, stock rooms and neighboring suites quickly. Call our 24/7 line first, then take the steps below. If water is near live electrical equipment or a sewage smell is strong, clear people from the area before anything else.

  1. Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or where the line enters the building.
  2. Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains if sewage is backing up.
  3. Cut power to wet areas only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.
  4. Move inventory, paper records and electronics above floor level.
  5. Take photos of the damage for your insurer before cleanup starts.

Finding a hidden leak in a business building

Hidden leaks show up as a climbing water bill, damp carpet, a warm spot on a slab or a meter that keeps moving when every fixture is off. We start by closing off sections of pipe to narrow the search, then use acoustic listening gear and pressure tests to pin the spot. That limits how much wall or floor has to be opened.

We keep this service modest. We find and fix leaks in supply lines, drains and fixtures. A full building survey across several structures is a different scale of work, and we will tell you if it is outside what we offer. Our leak detection page explains the method in more detail.

Drains, floor drains and grease

Floor drains in restrooms, mechanical rooms and kitchens take heavy use and rarely get attention until they overflow. A cable machine clears most blockages. If the same drain backs up again, a camera shows whether the pipe is cracked, bellied or full of roots. See our drain cleaning service for how that works.

Grease is a separate problem. Food-service drains collect fat that hardens inside the pipe, and a trap or interceptor catches some of it before it reaches the sewer. We can clear grease-clogged lines and look at your trap. Pumping out a large interceptor is often done by a hauler, so call and ask what fits your setup.

Backflow testing: a planned service

We are adding backflow assembly testing for business water lines and irrigation. It is not yet a standard visit, so call our 24/7 line to ask about availability before you book anything. If your water provider sent a notice, keep it handy. Rules on how often a device needs testing and who must do it vary, so ask your water provider or the Kentucky Division of Plumbing.

Why commercial plumbing bills vary

Two business calls that sound alike can differ a lot once the technician sees the building. The price comes after the diagnosis, not before.

Building size and layout

A long run to a back restroom takes more time than a short one beside the entrance.

Pipe material and age

Cast iron, galvanized and PVC all behave differently and need different parts and methods.

Access

Open ceilings and utility rooms are quick. Finished walls and concrete floors add labor.

Timing

Working at night or on a holiday to keep you open during the day affects the visit price.

Equipment needed

Cable machines, jetters and cameras each add setup time that depends on the line.

Business property and interruption policies often respond to sudden water damage, but gradual leaks and worn parts are usually excluded. Review your own policy and your lease, since responsibility may sit with the owner or the tenant.
