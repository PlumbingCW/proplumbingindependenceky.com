Why sewer pipes fail in the first place

Most failures come from a short list of causes. Knowing which one applies decides whether a small spot repair is enough.

Root intrusion

Roots enter at joints and cracks, then swell until the seal gives way. A split pipe often shows a root mass at its center.

Offset or separated joints

Soil shifts and two pipe sections slide apart. A lip forms that catches paper and grease, and soil can wash in.

Bellied pipe

A section sinks into soft ground and creates a low spot. Waste pools there and leaves a standing blockage.

Corroded cast iron

Older cast iron rusts from the inside, thins, and flakes. Large scale pieces break loose and jam.

Crushed or collapsed section

Heavy loads, tree movement, or construction can crush the pipe. Flow stops or runs through a gap.

Failed clay tile

Short clay sections have many joints. Every joint is a place for roots, gaps, or settling.

Backups that keep returning after cleaning

A line that opens with a cable and clogs again within weeks is not just dirty. Something is catching waste, and that something is usually damage. Our sewer line cleaning service restores flow, but cleaning cannot close a crack or flatten a sag.

Other hints include a sewer smell in the yard, a sunken stripe of lawn along the line, or a slow drain that clears only briefly. Gurgling toilets after rain can mean soil water is entering a break. Any of these justifies a camera look before spending more on cleaning. A soggy yard with no sewer smell may instead be the water supply, which our water line repair page covers.

What a technician finds with the camera

A sewer camera is a waterproof lens on a flexible rod, pushed down the line while a screen shows the inside of the pipe.

  • ✓Pipe material, such as PVC, cast iron, clay, or Orangeburg fiber pipe.
  • ✓Cracks, holes, and the type of break, whether lengthwise, circular, or shattered.
  • ✓Roots, how far they extend, and whether they enter at a joint.
  • ✓Offsets between pipe sections, measured against pipe width.
  • ✓Low spots where water stands instead of flowing.
  • ✓The depth and distance of each problem from the cleanout, in feet.
  • ✓A locator signal at the surface to mark the exact spot above ground.

Sewage backing up from a failed line

A broken sewer line can spill waste into the house or under the yard. Our 24/7 line takes calls at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows. The safest first steps are about limiting water and contact.

  1. Stop all water use, including laundry and dishwashers.
  2. Keep children, pets, and anyone ill away from wet areas.
  3. Do not use a wet vacuum on sewage or touch switches near water.
  4. Mark any wet spots in the yard, and take photos for insurance.
  5. Do not pour chemicals into the drains, as they can harm the pipe and the crew.
  6. Leave the cleanout capped unless the plumber tells you otherwise.

What decides the cost of a sewer repair

Sewer repair ranges from a short spot fix to a full replacement, and only the camera footage tells which one you face. These items drive the bill.

Depth of the pipe

A pipe 3 feet down is a simple dig. One 8 feet down needs shoring, more soil removal, and more time.

Length of damaged pipe

A 4-foot failed section is a different job from 40 feet of bad pipe.

What lies above

Concrete, a driveway, trees, or utility lines change digging time and restoration work.

Repair method

Spot repair, lining, bursting, and open trench each have different labor, material, and equipment needs.

Restoration scope

Backfill and surface repair depend on how much ground was opened.

Insurance often covers sudden damage such as a burst pipe or a resulting backup, but rarely covers repair of wear, age, or root damage. Read your own policy, and ask about sewer line or water backup endorsements.

Why checking a license matters before any dig

Sewer repair means opening ground and connecting to waste lines, so the work should follow state plumbing rules. Kentucky oversees plumbing through the Division of Plumbing inside the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction. Verify any plumber's license with that office, and ask the city or the Division about permit rules for your job.
